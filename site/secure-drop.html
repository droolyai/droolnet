Bring the truth.
Keep your agency.

If the public needs to know, evidence matters. This page explains the planned safety boundary for a future newsroom source system. It does not accept, encrypt, upload, send, or publish your material.

THE BOUNDARY This research page does not accept plaintext.

There is no recipient key, encryption runtime, receiver, or delivery path. Visiting the clearnet page can still expose network metadata, and this prototype makes no anonymity guarantee.

01 / BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Protect the source,
then protect the story.

01

Use a safer device

A work or managed device may record activity, filenames, clipboard contents, or screenshots. Use a device and network you trust.

02

Inspect your files

Photos, documents, and recordings can contain names, locations, author history, and device identifiers. This tool does not remove embedded metadata.

03

Share only what is needed

Never include a password, seed phrase, private key, or unrelated personal record. Encryption cannot undo an unsafe disclosure.

02 / BUILD THE PACKAGE

Document what happened.

Lead with what can be verified: what occurred, when, where, who can corroborate it, and what records support it. Separate what you observed from what you infer.

03 / WHAT THIS DOES NOT PROMISE

Encryption is protection.
It is not invisibility.

This clearnet research page does not accept, encrypt, upload, or download source material. It does not conceal your IP address, prevent device monitoring, sanitize files, guarantee editorial review, or provide a delivery channel.

A future encrypted envelope will still expose bounded routing metadata and padded size classes. Embedded document metadata remains visible to an authorized journalist after decryption. Browsers also cannot guarantee memory erasure or protection from extensions, input services, screenshots, or a compromised origin.

A future receiver must undergo independent security review, publish key rotation and retention policies, and provide a separate authenticated delivery path before the desk can be called operational.
